Every product has competitors. Pretending they don't exist doesn't make you less competitive; it makes you less informed. But building in response to competitors rather than in response to users is how products lose their distinctiveness.

Here's how we think about competitive positioning for each of the three products.

## Scoutr: the tools it's replacing

Scoutr competes with:
- **Manual eBay research.** Still the dominant approach for most resellers. Free, slow, error-prone.
- **Keepa and similar price tracking tools.** Good historical data, no AI analysis, no profitability assessment.
- **SellerAmp.** The closest direct competitor — a scanning tool for eBay and Amazon resellers with AI-assisted analysis.
- **Spreadsheet templates.** Custom-built fee calculators shared in reseller communities.

Our position: Scoutr is meaningfully better than spreadsheet templates and manual research (obvious), and we think it's better than SellerAmp for the specific eBay UK market because we've optimised for the UK fee structure and category taxonomy. SellerAmp's strength is Amazon; our strength is eBay.

The competitive moat we're building: data network effects. Every Scoutr scan refines our understanding of which products tend to be profitable in which categories. Over time, users who've been on the platform longer benefit from this accumulated signal.

**What we watch:** SellerAmp's feature releases, reseller community sentiment about scanning tools, whether any new entrants appear in the eBay-specific market.

**What we don't do:** race SellerAmp on features they're shipping. We ship what our users ask for.

## The Marqet: the freelance market incumbents

The Marqet competes with:
- **Fiverr and similar platforms.** Massive supply, inconsistent quality, race to the bottom on price, no eBay-specific focus.
- **Upwork.** More professional, project-based, higher friction, not eBay-specific.
- **Facebook groups and forums.** Where most eBay service transactions currently happen — informal, no payment escrow, no quality control.
- **Direct LinkedIn outreach.** How professional sellers find service providers — high friction, no discovery.

Our position: The Marqet is the only professional marketplace specifically for eBay and reseller services. Fiverr and Upwork have orders of magnitude more supply but minimal eBay-specific expertise. The Facebook group approach has no quality control, no payment protection, and no discovery mechanism.

The competitive moat: category specificity. Being the best place to find eBay-specific services is more valuable than being a general freelance marketplace for this use case.

**What we watch:** whether Fiverr creates an "eBay services" vertical (they create verticals frequently — it's worth watching). Whether a well-funded competitor enters the reseller services market specifically.

**What we don't do:** try to compete on breadth. We're not trying to list every type of freelance service; we're going deep on one category.

## The Hyve: the AI workspace market

The Hyve competes with:
- **Slack.** The dominant team communication platform. No native AI agent.
- **Notion.** Knowledge management with AI features. Not a real-time communication tool.
- **Linear.** Project management. Not a workspace.
- **Teams.** Enterprise-focused. Different market segment.
- **Various AI-first workspace startups.** Several have launched since GPT-4. Most have struggled to differentiate.

Our position: The Hyve is explicitly for small teams (under 20 people) building AI-native workflows. We're not trying to compete with Slack on communication; we're competing on AI context continuity. The differentiator: the AI agent in The Hyve actually remembers what your team has discussed and decided.

The competitive challenge: this market is crowded and moves fast. New AI workspace tools launch regularly. Our moat — if we can build it — is the quality of the AI context pipeline and the integration depth with the workflows these small teams run.

**What we watch:** every new AI workspace product launch. We use several of them ourselves to understand what's working and what isn't.

**What we do differently:** we're not trying to replace Slack. We're targeting teams who've decided they want AI embedded in their workflow and who find Slack's AI features inadequate. This is a subset of the market, but it's a growing subset.

## The general principle

The question we ask about any competitor: "What do they do well that we don't?" Not as a threat assessment — as an input to our product thinking.

SellerAmp does Amazon scanning well. That's useful context for thinking about whether we should add Amazon support to Scoutr (we haven't decided to). Fiverr has enormous supply in our category. That's useful context for thinking about how buyer trust in The Marqet's listing quality becomes a competitive advantage.

Competitors are sources of information. They're not dictators of what you build. Build for your users first, use competitor intelligence as one input among many.
