Pricing is one of the highest-leverage decisions in a SaaS product. A 10% improvement in pricing can have more impact than a 10% improvement in conversion. We've gotten it wrong twice.

Here's the story.

## Scoutr: the first model

Scoutr launched with usage-based pricing: £0.05 per scan, billed monthly. The logic was: charge for the value delivered — more scans, more value, more cost.

The problems:

**Anxiety.** Users who scanned frequently were watching their tab grow in real time. The psychological overhead of "each scan costs me money" changes behaviour — users became selective about scans in a way that reduced the product's value. The best resellers scan everything; anxiety about scan cost made them scan less.

**Unpredictability.** A month with a busy Saturday market and a slow weekday baseline produced wildly different bills. Users couldn't budget reliably.

**Low monetisation.** At £0.05 per scan, a user scanning 200 items per month (a reasonable power user) was paying £10/month. Not terrible, but the ceiling was low — the users most willing to pay were the heaviest users, and we'd already capped what they'd pay by anchoring to per-scan pricing.

## The second model: tiered by features

We moved to a tiered model:

- **Free:** 50 scans/month, basic confidence score
- **Pro (£9/month):** Unlimited scans, detailed fee breakdown, historical data
- **Team (£25/month):** Pro + multi-device, shared scan history

The problems:

**The free tier created a floor, not a funnel.** Users on free converted to paid slowly. The 50-scan limit hit heavy users but not the casual users who might convert on engagement.

**"Team" didn't resonate.** Reselling is a solo or small-team activity. "Team" sounded like enterprise. The £25 tier got minimal uptake.

**Decision fatigue.** Three tiers, with different feature unlocks, required users to understand what they were getting before they'd used the product enough to know what mattered.

## The third model: single flat price

Current model: £14.99/month, all features, unlimited scans. No tiers, no free tier (14-day free trial instead).

What changed:

**No anxiety.** Unlimited scans means users scan everything without worrying about cost. This is the behaviour that makes the product valuable.

**Predictable.** £14.99 every month, no surprises.

**No decision.** There's one option. The choice is: try it or don't.

**The trial converts better than the free tier.** A 14-day trial with full access creates urgency and shows the full value. A permanent free tier with limited features shows reduced value indefinitely.

## What the price communicates

Pricing communicates market positioning. £14.99/month positions Scoutr as a serious professional tool, not a toy. Resellers who take sourcing seriously will pay £14.99 for a tool that saves them money and time on every sourcing run.

The previous £9/month "Pro" tier communicated something cheaper. Users who paid £9 had different expectations than users who pay £15. The small price increase filtered for more serious users and reduced support overhead.

## The Marqet pricing

The Marqet has a different pricing structure: the platform takes a 8% fee on completed transactions. No monthly subscription for buyers or sellers.

The reasoning:

**Zero friction to join.** Sellers can list for free; buyers can browse and enquire for free. The platform only earns when a transaction succeeds. This aligns incentives: we make money when our users make money.

**Self-funding.** A successful transaction generates revenue without any separate billing flow. Sellers receive 92% of the agreed price; 8% is retained automatically.

**Scale-appropriate.** At low transaction volume, a subscription would produce negligible revenue. Transaction fees grow with the platform's success.

The tradeoff: transaction fees are invisible until the first transaction, which can create negative surprise. We're explicit about the fee structure in onboarding and in the listing creation flow.

## The Hyve pricing

The Hyve is in the process of moving from a free beta to a paid model. The target: per-workspace pricing, with a free tier for small teams (up to 5 members) and a flat £29/month for larger workspaces.

The logic: workspace pricing is more intuitive than per-seat pricing for a team tool. The decision-maker (the person paying) thinks of the workspace as a unit, not as N individual seats. Flat workspace pricing simplifies the billing conversation.

## The lesson

Simple pricing outperforms complex pricing at our stage. Not because simple is always right — at enterprise scale, per-seat pricing with volume discounts is often correct — but because we don't have the sales infrastructure to explain complex pricing, and our users make fast decisions.

A user evaluating Scoutr for 10 minutes will understand "£14.99/month, unlimited, 14-day free trial" immediately. They will not take the time to understand the implications of feature gating across three tiers.

Match pricing complexity to the sales process. When the sales process is "user sees the site, signs up, and decides within a day", keep pricing simple.
