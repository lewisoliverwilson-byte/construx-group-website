The Marqet has processed 180+ transactions across brand design, copywriting, development, marketing, and consulting. Price is the most consequential variable in whether a buyer completes a purchase. Here's what the data and patterns have taught us.

## Higher prices convert better in some categories

The conventional wisdom — lower prices attract more buyers — is wrong in professional services.

In The Marqet's brand design category, listings priced between £800-1,200 convert at nearly double the rate of listings priced £200-400. The explanation is credibility: buyers in this category are evaluating unfamiliar sellers. Low prices signal low quality, inexperience, or desperation. A price at the professional market rate signals "this person knows what their work is worth."

The pattern doesn't hold everywhere. In commodity categories (basic social media graphics, simple data entry tasks), lower prices do convert better — the buyer is optimising for cost rather than quality signals.

The pricing advice for sellers: research the market rate for your category and price at or slightly above it. Don't underprice to attract early clients. Underpricing attracts price-sensitive clients who are the worst clients for a new marketplace relationship.

## Packages outperform hourly rates

Listings structured as packages (defined deliverables, fixed price) convert at 2.3x the rate of listings offering hourly work.

The reasons are clear from the buyer's perspective:

**Uncertainty elimination:** Hourly engagements have an uncertain total cost. The buyer commits to £100/hour without knowing if they'll need 5 hours or 50. Packages remove this uncertainty — £800 for a brand identity package is a known cost.

**Scope clarity:** A package defines what's delivered. Hourly listings require the buyer to define the scope, which most buyers don't know how to do for specialist services they're buying infrequently.

**Perceived value:** A named package ("Brand Identity Package: logo + color system + typography guide") feels more tangible than "x hours of design work." The name creates a mental model for what the buyer is purchasing.

Sellers who convert their hourly listings to packages see immediate improvements in inquiry rate and conversion.

## The £ anchor effect

Listings that show multiple tiers (Starter / Standard / Premium) outperform single-price listings. The buyer's eye goes to the middle tier — the anchor creates the reference point that makes the middle option feel like the right choice.

```
Starter      £400   Logo only
Standard     £800   Logo + brand guide       ← most selected
Premium      £1,500  Logo + brand guide + social templates
```

This is the decoy effect in action. The Premium tier makes Standard look affordable; the Starter tier makes Standard look comprehensive. We've seen sellers increase revenue by 35% by restructuring a single-price listing into a three-tier package with the same total offering.

## What the 15% deposit model does to psychology

The Marqet operates on escrow — the buyer pays in full at engagement, held until completion. We considered a 15% deposit model (buyer pays 15% upfront, remainder on completion) but decided against it for three reasons:

1. **Complexity:** Two payment events is twice the friction
2. **Commitment signal:** Full payment upfront is a stronger commitment signal for both parties — the seller knows the buyer is serious, the buyer feels invested in a good outcome
3. **Dispute reduction:** Partial payment models create disputes at the boundary ("the 15% doesn't cover the revisions you asked for")

The one case where a deposit model would be better: very long engagements (multi-month projects) where cashflow matters to the seller. We'll add payment milestones for projects over 30 days in a future version.

## The free scope problem

Buyers frequently try to expand scope informally — asking for "just one more thing" outside the defined package. This is the professional services equivalent of scope creep.

The Marqet's messaging system makes scope conversations visible and documentable. We've added a "scope change" flow that allows sellers to propose a paid scope extension:

```
Seller: "You've asked for an additional color variant — I can add this for £150.
         Accept the scope change to proceed."
Buyer: [Accept | Decline]
```

The explicit flow stops informal scope expansion. Early sellers were reluctant to use it (worried about seeming difficult) but it's now used in 12% of engagements — and those engagements have a higher satisfaction rate than those without scope changes, presumably because the relationship dynamics are clearer.

## What drives repeat purchase

41% of buyers return for a second engagement. The highest correlates with repeat purchase:

1. **Response time < 4 hours** — sellers who respond to buyer messages quickly within the first day of an engagement see 2x the repeat rate
2. **Delivery before deadline** — buyers who receive work ahead of the stated deadline are significantly more likely to return
3. **One revision cycle** — engagements that require only one round of revisions (rather than three or four) have higher repeat rates

The interesting finding: raw quality of the deliverable (as rated by buyers) is a weaker predictor of repeat purchase than speed and revision count. A buyer who gets good work delivered on time with one clean revision cycle is more likely to return than a buyer who gets excellent work that required three revision cycles.

The implication for sellers: invest in upfront discovery (understanding exactly what the buyer wants before starting) more than in unlimited revision rounds after the fact.
