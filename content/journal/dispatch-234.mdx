We have three live products. We're considering a fourth: The Lattice, a professional network for people working in AI. Before writing a line of code, we ran a demand test.

This is the methodology. The data at day 30 is included. The decision criteria is written down before we look at the data.

## The idea

The Marqet connects buyers with AI professionals for project work. The Lattice is a different surface: a standing network for AI practitioners to find each other, share work in progress, and discover opportunities over time.

The difference from The Marqet: The Marqet is transactional (you hire someone for a specific project). The Lattice is relational (you follow someone's work, they follow yours, patterns emerge over time).

The difference from LinkedIn: LinkedIn is optimised for employment search and B2B outreach. The Lattice would be optimised for practitioners — a community where the output is as visible as the person.

## Why run a demand test before building

Building a professional network is expensive. The cold-start problem for a two-sided marketplace is hard; for a network where value comes from who else is in it, it's harder. The Lattice only works if enough of the right people join and are active.

You can't validate a network with a landing page and a "Join the waitlist" button. Anyone will give you their email for a vague promise. The question is whether people are interested enough to engage in the absence of the full product.

So we tested three specific behaviours before building:

1. **Joining the waiting list after reading a substantive description of the product** (not a vague teaser)
2. **Sharing the list with someone else** (referral behaviour, not just passive signup)
3. **Returning to engage with a follow-up email** (signals ongoing interest, not one-time curiosity)

## The demand test structure

**The waiting list:** A page at construxgroup.io/the-lattice with a detailed description of the product — what it would do, who it's for, what "active on The Lattice" looks like in practice. Not a vague "professional network for AI practitioners" — a specific description of features, community structure, and what participating would involve.

**The journal series:** Three dispatches on The Lattice concept, published three weeks apart. Not "sign up for our new product" posts — substantive posts about the problem the product solves. The dispatches drive organic traffic from readers who don't know The Lattice is coming.

**The threshold:** We set a threshold before looking at the data. If we hit 500 signups with a referral rate above 15% and an email return rate above 25%, we build. If we don't hit 500 in 60 days, we don't. No exceptions, no retrospective adjustments to the threshold.

## Day 30 data

At day 30, mid-test:

- **Signups:** 284 (57% of the 500 threshold at the 50% mark)
- **Referral rate:** 18.2% (signups who came via a referred link, not organic or direct)
- **Email return rate:** 31.4% (signups who opened and clicked through to the follow-up dispatch)

The pace is slightly behind linear trajectory for 500 — we'd need 216 more signups in 30 days to hit the threshold. But the referral rate and email return rate are above target.

## What the data says

The quality indicators (referral rate, email return rate) are strong. The quantity indicator (total signups) is on the edge. This suggests the people who are interested are genuinely interested — they're sharing the link, they're returning for the follow-up — but the audience is more concentrated than we assumed.

If we hit 500 by day 60, the demand signal is strong: 500 signups, high referral quality, high engagement. Build with confidence.

If we finish at 350-400, the decision is harder. That's enough to build a meaningful initial network, but it's below the threshold we set. We don't adjust the threshold post-hoc. We don't build.

If we finish at 350-400 we could run a second test at 90 days. But that's a different test with a different result. The original 60-day test would have returned a "don't build" signal, and that matters.

## The important part: the pre-committed threshold

The pre-committed threshold is the whole point of the demand test. If you look at your data and then decide what threshold would justify building, you'll always justify building. Founders are optimistic by nature; every data set looks good if you're generating the criteria after seeing it.

We set 500 signups because it's a number that represents meaningful demonstrated interest in a market we understand. 284 people reading a detailed description of The Lattice and choosing to be on the waiting list is interesting. 500 is compelling. 350 is... interesting.

The threshold creates a pre-commitment device against founder self-delusion. Either the demand is there or it isn't. We find out in 30 days.
