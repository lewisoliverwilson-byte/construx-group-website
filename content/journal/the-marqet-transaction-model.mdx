The hardest part of building a marketplace isn't the discovery or the listings — it's money. Specifically: when does money move, and what happens when a transaction goes wrong?

Every marketplace eventually faces a buyer who says they didn't receive the service they paid for, and a seller who says they delivered it. The payment model determines who's right by default.

## The payment timing options

Three common approaches:

**Pay-then-deliver.** Buyer pays upfront; seller delivers; money releases. The buyer has risk: if the seller doesn't deliver, the buyer has to dispute. The seller has certainty: they know the money is there before they work.

**Deliver-then-pay.** Seller delivers; buyer pays. The seller has risk: if the buyer doesn't pay, the seller has done the work for nothing. The buyer has certainty: they can evaluate the delivery before paying.

**Escrow.** Buyer pays into an escrow account; seller delivers; buyer confirms delivery; money releases. Both parties have protection: the buyer knows their money is secure; the seller knows the money exists before they work. Disputes are resolved by the platform from the escrow balance.

The Marqet uses a modified escrow model.

## Our escrow implementation

When a buyer purchases a service:
1. Stripe processes the payment and holds the funds.
2. We create a `Transaction` record with status `escrow`.
3. The seller receives a notification and can begin the work.
4. When the seller marks the work complete, the buyer receives a notification.
5. The buyer has 72 hours to confirm delivery or raise a dispute.
6. If the buyer confirms, we release payment to the seller (via Stripe's transfer API).
7. If the buyer doesn't respond within 72 hours, the transaction auto-confirms and releases.
8. If the buyer raises a dispute, the transaction enters manual review.

```ts
type TransactionStatus =
  | 'pending'     // payment processing
  | 'escrow'      // payment held, work not started
  | 'in_progress' // seller acknowledged, working
  | 'delivered'   // seller marked complete
  | 'confirmed'   // buyer confirmed, payment released
  | 'disputed'    // buyer raised dispute, manual review
  | 'refunded'    // resolved in buyer's favour
  | 'cancelled';  // cancelled before work started
```

The 72-hour auto-confirm is important. Without it, sellers are dependent on buyers actively clicking "confirm delivery" to receive payment. Buyers are busy; many would never click the button, leaving sellers in a perpetual waiting state. The auto-confirm solves this: sellers are paid within 72 hours of marking delivery complete, regardless of buyer response.

## The Stripe implementation

Stripe handles escrow via payment intents with manual capture:

```ts
// When buyer purchases:
const paymentIntent = await stripe.paymentIntents.create({
  amount: listing.price_pence,
  currency: 'gbp',
  capture_method: 'manual', // don't capture yet
  metadata: {
    transactionId: transaction.id,
    listingId: listing.id,
    buyerId: buyer.id,
    sellerId: listing.seller_id,
  },
});

// When buyer confirms delivery:
await stripe.paymentIntents.capture(transaction.stripe_payment_intent_id);

// After capture: transfer to seller's connected account
await stripe.transfers.create({
  amount: sellerAmount, // listing price minus platform commission
  currency: 'gbp',
  destination: seller.stripe_account_id,
  transfer_group: transaction.id,
});
```

The `capture_method: 'manual'` instructs Stripe to authorise the card but not capture the funds. The buyer's card is charged the hold immediately (it shows on their statement), but the actual funds don't leave until `capture` is called. Stripe holds payment intents in the uncaptured state for up to 7 days — enough for our 72-hour window.

For marketplace payments that need to last longer than 7 days, Stripe offers connect with separate charges and transfers, which we'd use for services that take more than a week to deliver.

## The dispute process

Disputes are handled manually at our current scale. When a buyer raises a dispute:

1. We freeze the transaction — the escrow funds stay in Stripe's hold.
2. We send both parties a standard questionnaire: what was agreed, what was delivered, what's missing.
3. We review the listing description, any messages between buyer and seller (if they used our in-platform messaging), and the seller's delivery notification.
4. We make a determination: release to seller, partial refund, full refund.

The adjudication criteria are explicitly listed in our seller terms and buyer terms. "I changed my mind" is not grounds for a refund once delivery is confirmed. "The delivery didn't match the listing description" is grounds for a refund.

At our current scale (40 sellers, ~50 transactions per month), we handle disputes manually. We've had 6 disputes in our first three months of operation. 4 were resolved in the buyer's favour (service wasn't delivered as described). 2 were resolved in the seller's favour (buyer claimed non-delivery; seller had proof of delivery).

## Platform commission

We take 8% of each transaction (on the commission tier). The seller receives 92% of the listing price. The 8% covers platform operations, payment processing fees (Stripe takes ~1.4% + 20p), and dispute resolution overhead.

The commission is taken during the transfer step:

```ts
const platformFee = Math.round(transaction.amount * 0.08);
const sellerAmount = transaction.amount - platformFee;

// Transfer seller's portion to their connected account
await stripe.transfers.create({
  amount: sellerAmount,
  currency: 'gbp',
  destination: seller.stripe_account_id,
});

// Platform fee stays in our Stripe balance automatically
```

The Stripe balance accumulates our commission; we pay it out to our bank account on a monthly schedule.

## The seller onboarding requirement

To receive payments, sellers must complete Stripe Connect onboarding — providing identity verification, bank account details, and business information to satisfy Stripe's KYC requirements.

This is a significant friction point for new sellers. Several potential sellers have dropped off at the Stripe Connect step. We've tried to reduce this friction by:

1. Making the requirement explicit at listing creation time ("to list services, you'll need to complete payment setup")
2. Starting the Stripe Connect flow immediately after the first listing is published
3. Providing a clear "you're not yet set up to receive payments" banner for sellers who haven't completed onboarding

The KYC requirement isn't optional — Stripe requires it, and rightly so. The friction is unavoidable. We try to make the requirement expected and the process as clear as possible.
