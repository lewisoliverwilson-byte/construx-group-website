Three ventures are live and generating revenue. We're thinking about a fourth. Here's where the thinking currently sits — the idea, the market, and the honest assessment of whether to build it.

## The idea

A professional network for AI practitioners — researchers, engineers, product managers, and operators working with AI systems at the frontier.

The form: structured profiles (not CVs, not LinkedIn bios — structured descriptions of specific AI work done), private discussion channels, and a job board for AI roles.

The name we're considering: **The Lattice**.

## The problem

LinkedIn is where AI professionals are. LinkedIn is also where everyone else is — and the signal-to-noise for AI-specific professional activity is low. The feed is full of product launches, thought leadership content, and networking content that isn't relevant to practitioners who want to connect with other practitioners.

Twitter/X has the AI research community but the format is unfavourable for structured professional representation and private professional discussion.

There's a gap: a space for AI practitioners that's structured enough to signal professional credibility and private enough for substantive discussion.

## The market

AI practitioners are a growing, high-value demographic. The number of people working professionally with AI systems is increasing faster than any comparable technical field. They have specific professional networking needs that general platforms don't serve well.

The market is well-defined. The question is whether it's addressable — whether AI practitioners would adopt a new professional platform, given the existing alternatives.

## The honest assessment

**For:** The Hyve gives us experience with team communication and real-time systems. The Marqet gives us experience with marketplace dynamics and trust verification. Both are directionally relevant to a professional network.

**Against:** Building a professional network is a cold-start problem. A network with few members isn't useful to any member. The minimum viable network is larger than the minimum viable product for any of our other ventures.

**The demand question:** We'd need AI practitioners to explicitly choose The Lattice over LinkedIn for professional representation, and over private Discord/Slack communities for discussion. The case for this switch isn't obvious — the switching cost is real and the benefit requires enough members to matter.

**The resource question:** Building a fourth venture in parallel with three live products is a context-switching cost that's easy to underestimate. The Hyve still requires significant development investment. The Marqet is in an early-growth phase. Adding a fourth product now may diffuse attention in a way that hurts all four.

## What we're doing instead of deciding

We're running a lightweight test before committing to the build:

- Build a simple landing page for The Lattice explaining the concept
- Collect email signups from AI practitioners via targeted promotion
- Target: 500 signups in 60 days as a demand signal

If we hit 500 signups from practitioners (not just curious people), we have evidence that the problem resonates. If we don't, we have evidence that the framing doesn't land, and we can adjust before committing to a full build.

The test costs: a week of time to build the landing page and write the copy. The cost of not doing the test: building a fourth venture based on intuition rather than data.

## The meta-lesson

The venture we're most tempted to build is always the one with the most interesting problem. The interesting problem isn't always the right problem to build next.

The discipline of doing a lightweight demand test before committing to a build is the thing that prevents building products for no one. The Marqet started with a waitlist before we wrote a line of product code. Scoutr started with a manual prototype before we built the automated version. The Hyve had 20 beta users before we built features.

The Lattice will follow the same pattern or it won't happen. We'll know in 60 days.
