Transactional email — password resets, subscription confirmations, receipts, welcome emails — is critical infrastructure for a SaaS product. If these emails land in spam or don't arrive at all, users can't access their accounts and trust erodes quickly.

Getting email delivered reliably requires understanding a few technical pieces that most small teams treat as an afterthought until something breaks.

## The problem with raw SMTP

Sending email via raw SMTP (connecting directly to port 25 on a mail server) is a deliverability disaster for a new sender. Email providers (Gmail, Outlook, Yahoo) use reputation signals to determine whether email should be delivered or marked as spam. A new sender has no reputation — and new senders who send cold email are often spammers.

The reputation signals include:
- IP address reputation (is this IP on a blocklist?)
- Domain reputation (how long has this domain been sending email? Do recipients engage?)
- Authentication (SPF, DKIM, DMARC alignment)
- Content signals (spam-trigger phrases, link patterns, image-to-text ratio)
- Engagement (do recipients open? Reply? Delete without reading?)

Building email reputation from scratch takes months. For a SaaS product that needs transactional email from day one, this is not a viable approach.

## Resend: the tool we use

We use Resend for all transactional email across the three products. Resend provides:

- SMTP relay through their infrastructure (which has established reputation)
- SDK for Node.js that works with Next.js Server Actions and API routes
- React email templates (write your email in JSX, Resend renders and sends it)
- Dashboard with delivery rate, open rate, and bounce tracking
- SPF and DKIM authentication through their domain setup

The integration is a single SDK call:

```typescript
import { Resend } from 'resend';

const resend = new Resend(process.env.RESEND_API_KEY);

await resend.emails.send({
  from: 'hello@construxgroup.io',
  to: user.email,
  subject: 'Welcome to Scoutr',
  react: WelcomeEmail({ userName: user.name }),
});
```

React email templates are written as React components with inline styles (email clients strip external stylesheets). The same mental model as UI components, but for email HTML that has to work in Outlook 2016.

## SPF, DKIM, and DMARC

These three DNS records are required for reliable email delivery.

**SPF (Sender Policy Framework)**: a DNS TXT record that specifies which IP addresses are authorised to send email for your domain. When we send email through Resend, the receiving mail server checks whether Resend's IP addresses are listed in our SPF record.

```
v=spf1 include:amazonses.com include:_spf.resend.com ~all
```

**DKIM (DomainKeys Identified Mail)**: adds a cryptographic signature to outgoing email that proves the email was sent by someone with access to your domain's private key. Recipients can verify the signature using the public key in your DNS. This verifies that the email wasn't modified in transit.

Resend provides a CNAME record to add to your DNS. This authorises Resend to sign email on your behalf with a key they manage.

**DMARC (Domain-based Message Authentication, Reporting and Conformance)**: a policy that tells receiving mail servers what to do when SPF and DKIM checks fail. Our DMARC record:

```
v=DMARC1; p=none; rua=mailto:dmarc@construxgroup.io
```

`p=none` is the monitoring mode — email that fails authentication is still delivered, but failures are reported. As our email program matures and we're confident in authentication, we'll move to `p=quarantine` (failed email goes to spam) and eventually `p=reject` (failed email is dropped).

The rua address receives aggregate reports about authentication failures from receiving mail servers. Reading these reports reveals misconfiguration before it causes delivery problems.

## The construxgroup.io gap we haven't fixed

The contact form on the Construx Group website submits successfully but doesn't send an email — we haven't configured the RESEND_API_KEY in the Amplify environment variables for this app yet.

This is a known gap tracked in the now page. The form submission stores the contact data, but the email notification to us is missing. Users who submit the form don't get a confirmation email, and we don't get a notification.

Fix: add RESEND_API_KEY to the Amplify environment variables and NEXT_PUBLIC_SITE_URL while we're there.

## The deliverability monitoring habit

After setting up authentication, the ongoing practice: check Resend's delivery dashboard weekly. Key metrics:

**Delivery rate**: the percentage of sent emails that were accepted by the recipient's mail server. Below 95% indicates a problem.

**Bounce rate**: hard bounces (invalid addresses) accumulate over time and hurt sender reputation. Keep the hard bounce rate below 2%. Clean your email list regularly.

**Spam complaint rate**: if recipients mark your email as spam, Gmail reports this through Google Postmaster Tools. Keep complaint rate below 0.1%.

**Open rate**: a weak signal (Apple Mail's privacy protection has made open rate less reliable), but large drops indicate emails landing in spam.

Most transactional email problems are caught early by monitoring these metrics and acting before the reputation damage compounds.
