There's a version of "AI-first" that means you use Copilot to autocomplete your code. There's another version that means the AI is structurally embedded in every layer of the product — the design process, the research phase, the architecture decisions, the user-facing experience. We mean the second thing.

## What we actually mean by AI-first

When we start a new venture, Claude isn't a tool we reach for when we're stuck. It's in the room from the beginning — helping define what we're building, surfacing the edge cases we'd miss, writing the first drafts of copy and code that we then sharpen. The output is never shipped raw, but the speed at which we reach a sharpened first draft is qualitatively different from how a traditional studio works.

The practical result: a two-person team can move with the output velocity of a ten-person team. Not by cutting corners, but by eliminating the dead time between "I have an idea" and "I have a working thing to react to."

## Why Anthropic and Claude specifically

We've used the frontier models. We chose Claude for three reasons that matter for product work.

**Instruction-following that holds.** Long, precise system prompts that constrain behaviour in specific ways actually constrain behaviour in Claude. This is not always true of other models. For products where the AI's output is the product — like The Marqet — this reliability is load-bearing.

**Writing quality at the output layer.** Claude produces prose that doesn't read like a machine wrote it. For a group that cares about brand voice across every venture, this matters more than it might seem.

**The tooling ecosystem.** Claude Code, MCP servers, Claude Projects, the Anthropic SDK — these form a coherent developer platform that we can build on top of, not just prompt into. Our tools run on tools that run on Claude.

## The honest trade-offs

This approach has constraints worth naming.

We move fast but we move in the direction the technology is good at. Problems that require massive proprietary datasets, problems that are fundamentally human in a way AI can't proxy, problems that depend on hardware — these aren't in our portfolio, at least not yet.

We're also genuinely dependent on Anthropic's roadmap. We think that's a reasonable bet given where the technology is heading, but we don't pretend the dependency doesn't exist.

## What this means for the portfolio

Every venture in the Construx portfolio was conceived because AI made it tractable. Scoutr needed the ability to scan and reason about large numbers of product listings in real time — impractical to hand-code heuristics for, trivial for Claude. The Marqet needed a way to organise and surface hundreds of AI configurations in a useful taxonomy — Claude helped build and test the taxonomy. The Hyve needed an AI team member that could hold the context of an entire project — only possible with the context windows and instruction fidelity of modern frontier models.

None of these ventures would exist without the current state of the technology. That's the thesis. Not that AI helps us build things faster, but that AI *defines* what things are now possible to build.

We're building the things.
