A few months into building the Construx Group website, I removed the pricing page.

It had been there. I'd spent time thinking about the pricing model, writing out the tiers, and building the page. Then I looked at it and concluded it wasn't doing what I wanted it to do, and removed it.

Here's why.

## What a pricing page does

A pricing page is a conversion funnel element. It answers "how much does this cost?" before the prospect has to ask, and it removes a barrier to entry by letting people self-qualify. If you're under the budget threshold, you proceed. If you're over it, you leave.

For a productised service or SaaS product with fixed, predictable pricing, a pricing page is clearly right. The person browsing needs to know: does this fit my budget before I invest time in a call?

## Why it didn't work for Construx client work

Our client work isn't fixed-price in the sense that there's a menu with predetermined costs. It's project-based. A project to build a customer-facing AI product from scratch is priced differently from a project to add AI capabilities to an existing product. The range is too wide for a pricing page to be honest.

A pricing page with a wide range ("£15,000 – £150,000") is nearly useless — the range is so large it tells the prospect nothing. A pricing page with specific prices forces you to commit to numbers before you know the scope.

The pricing page I built fell into the wide-range trap. It listed categories ("Prototypes from £X, Full builds from £Y") but the numbers were approximations that weren't particularly accurate for any specific project. I was showing approximations dressed up as precision. That's worse than not showing numbers.

## What we put in its place

Rather than a pricing page, we focus on:

**Proof of work:** What we've built, how fast, and what the results were. The work-with-us page has case studies of the three Construx products — what they are, what they took to build, and what makes them impressive technically. This answers the real question: "Can these people actually build the thing I need?"

**Process transparency:** How an engagement works, step by step. Discovery call → proposal → build → ship. The proposal step is where pricing happens — after we understand the scope. This is honest. The price for a project can't be determined before the scope is determined.

**Contact-first:** The call to action is "start the conversation," not "pick a plan." The first conversation is free. The proposal comes after.

## The conversation about pricing

When someone contacts us and asks "how much does it cost?", we have a real conversation about scope before we answer.

This conversation has several useful properties:

1. **It qualifies the prospect.** Someone willing to have a 45-minute discovery call before seeing a number is a more serious prospect than someone who wants to comparison-shop on a pricing page.

2. **It allows accurate scoping.** The price we quote after a discovery call is accurate. The price on a pricing page is necessarily an estimate.

3. **It creates a relationship.** A conversation builds more trust than a pricing table. We're selling a relationship (working together for 6-12 weeks) not a commodity (a defined number of units at a fixed price).

## What this costs

Not having a pricing page loses some prospects who won't engage without seeing a number first. There are people who would be good clients who don't contact us because we haven't answered the budget question upfront.

This is a real cost. We accept it.

The alternative — publishing prices that are necessarily approximate or ranges too wide to be useful — doesn't solve the problem. It creates the appearance of transparency without the substance.

## The SaaS products are different

The individual products (Scoutr, The Marqet, The Hyve) have pricing pages. SaaS pricing is fixed, predictable, and self-serve — the context where a pricing page genuinely helps prospects self-qualify.

For a professional services engagement, the context is different. The engagement is not a commodity, the price depends on scope, and the most valuable thing we can do for a serious prospect is understand their problem before we quote it.

The pricing conversation happens. It just happens in a proposal, not on a web page.
