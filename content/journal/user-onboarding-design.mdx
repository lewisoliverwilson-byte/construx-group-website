Onboarding is the most important part of a SaaS product that most teams under-invest in. The activation moment — the point where a user understands and experiences the product's core value — determines whether they stay or churn. Products with unclear activation moments have high early churn regardless of their quality.

Here's how we think about and design onboarding across three products with fundamentally different activation criteria.

## Activation looks different for every product

Before you can design onboarding, you have to define what activation means for your specific product. Activation is not signing up, not completing a profile, not clicking around the product. Activation is the moment where the user experiences the core value proposition for the first time.

For Scoutr, activation is: the user performs their first product scan and sees a fee calculation for a real product they're considering. That's the moment. Everything before it is pre-activation.

For The Marqet, activation is: the user views a listing that matches their professional role and reads far enough to understand its specific value. (Purchase is a stronger activation signal, but it's a later moment.)

For The Hyve, activation is: the user creates a workspace, invites a collaborator (or themselves with a second account), and experiences the AI agent responding to a real project question. This is a more complex activation moment that requires more setup.

Defining activation precisely changes how you design onboarding. Every onboarding step should be evaluated against: "does this get the user closer to the activation moment, or is it overhead?"

## The Scoutr onboarding flow

Scoutr's activation is low-friction by design. No profile setup, no preferences survey, no feature tour. Sign up, land on the scan interface, paste a product URL, get results.

The first-time experience:
1. Sign up (email + password, or OAuth)
2. Redirect directly to the scan interface (not a dashboard, not a "getting started" page)
3. Explanatory microcopy at the top: "Paste any Amazon, eBay, or B&M product URL to calculate profit margins"
4. An example product URL is pre-populated — click "Scan" to see how it works before entering your own

The pre-populated example was the most impactful onboarding change we made. Before it, activation required users to have a product URL ready. After it, users could experience the core product without any preparation. Time-to-activation dropped from "several minutes" to "under 60 seconds."

The lesson: remove everything that stands between the new user and the activation moment. Reduce friction to its minimum viable form.

## The Marqet onboarding flow

The Marqet's challenge: 167 listings across many professional categories. The activation moment (a user finding a relevant listing) requires them to either know what they're looking for or discover it through browse or search.

We designed around role-based filtering. On first visit (no session):
1. A modal overlay: "What best describes your work?" with 8 professional role options
2. The browse interface immediately filters to show listings relevant to that role
3. A spotlight on the 3 most-purchased listings in that role category

This converts an intimidating 167-listing catalogue into a curated, relevant subset. Users who answer the role question reach the activation moment (a relevant listing) in an average of 90 seconds. Users who skip it take over 3 minutes on average, with higher bounce rates.

The role selection also serves personalisation: subsequent visits surface role-relevant new listings first.

## The Hyve onboarding: the multi-step problem

The Hyve's activation requires more setup than the other two products. A user can't experience collaborative AI without a workspace, without at least understanding the channel metaphor, and without interacting with the AI agent.

We handle this with a guided setup that's designed to feel like using the product rather than setting it up:

1. **Create workspace** — one field, no friction
2. **Name your first channel** — immediately framed as "start with the project you're working on now"
3. **First message prompt** — the channel is pre-populated with a prompt: "Describe your project — what are you trying to build?" The user types their answer; the AI responds in the channel. This is the activation moment.

The key design decision: the guided setup IS the first AI interaction. Users don't go through setup and then use the product. They experience the product as part of setup.

This reduces the psychological barrier of "I need to set everything up before I can use it." The first AI response they see is a response to their own project description.

## Onboarding anti-patterns we've avoided

**The feature tour.** Tooltips that walk through every feature on first visit. Users skip these. By the time they reach the feature that matters, they've tuned out. Show features in context, not in sequence.

**The preference survey.** Asking users what they want before showing them the product. This delays activation. Ask preferences in the context of using the product, or infer them from behaviour.

**The empty state.** A dashboard with no content is disorienting. The user doesn't know what to do. Every empty state should have a specific, prominent call to action that leads directly toward the activation moment.

**The confirmation email gate.** Requiring email confirmation before accessing the product. We verify email eventually (for billing), but don't gate product access behind it. Let users reach activation first; verification can happen in the background.

## Measuring onboarding quality

The metric we track for onboarding: activation rate (users who reach the activation moment within 24 hours of signup) and time-to-activation.

Activation rate tells you what percentage of your signups are reaching the value moment. Time-to-activation tells you how much friction you're imposing.

Neither metric alone is sufficient. High activation rate with long time-to-activation means users eventually get there but the path is too slow. Low activation rate with fast time-to-activation means users aren't finding the value even when they reach it quickly — which is a product problem, not an onboarding problem.

The target: activation rate above 60% within 24 hours, time-to-activation under 5 minutes for products where that's achievable. Scoutr is at 74% and 2 minutes. The Marqet is at 58% and 4 minutes. The Hyve's activation is harder to measure precisely (the activation moment is more subjective) but improving.
