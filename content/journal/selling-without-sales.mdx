Scoutr is a B2B SaaS tool. The buyer is a professional reseller or a small business with a product sourcing workflow. This is, by the conventional playbook, the kind of product that gets sold — demos, follow-up calls, proposals, a dedicated account executive.

We have none of that. Here's how acquisition and conversion actually work.

## The product-led growth assumption

We built Scoutr on the assumption that the product should do the selling. The model: users find the product (via search, content, community), sign up for a free trial, use the product, and either see the value and pay or don't.

This assumption has a prerequisite: the product must be able to demonstrate its value within the trial period without human assistance. If the product requires explanation, guidance, or a demo to show value, PLG fails.

Scoutr was designed to meet this prerequisite. The scan interface is immediately understandable: paste a URL, click scan, see fees and profit margins. No onboarding call needed. No feature tour. A new user can complete a full scan workflow in under 2 minutes.

## What actually drives acquisition

Three sources account for most Scoutr signups:

**Organic search** — posts about Amazon FBA fee calculation, retail arbitrage margin calculation, and product scanning for resellers find Scoutr via Google. These are high-intent searches from people who have the problem Scoutr solves.

The journal has contributed to this. Posts that include specific details about fee structures, profit margin calculation, and product sourcing workflows rank for long-tail searches. These aren't viral posts; they're discoverable posts by the right people.

**Reddit and reseller communities** — Amazon FBA subreddits, reseller forums, and Facebook groups discuss tools regularly. Scoutr has been mentioned organically in these communities by users who find it useful. This is not something we actively manage; it emerges when the product genuinely solves the problem.

**Word of mouth** — professional resellers who use multiple tools and talk to each other. The referral rate is hard to measure precisely (we ask users how they found us), but roughly 15-20% of signups report hearing about Scoutr from another reseller.

## How trial conversion works

The trial is 14 days, unlimited scans. We don't gate features or add artificial limits. The trial is the full product.

The conversion message at day 10 of the trial is simple: "Your trial ends in 4 days. Continue with Professional for £35/month or Starter for £15/month."

There's no sales call. No demo offer. No "let's chat" CTA. Just a clear path to continue.

Trial-to-paid conversion is around 22%. This is reasonable for a self-serve B2B tool; enterprise tools with active sales support often convert higher, but the CAC (customer acquisition cost) is also much higher.

## The counter-argument: what we're leaving on the table

The honest accounting of what product-led growth without sales loses:

**Larger deal sizes.** An outbound sales motion can surface upsell opportunities — teams with multiple users, organizations with multiple resellers, enterprise accounts with bulk requirements. PLG converts individuals; sales can convert organizations. We are leaving enterprise-sized deals on the table.

**Faster feedback loops.** Sales calls are product research. The objections a prospect raises, the features they ask about, the competitors they mention — all signal. Without sales calls, we learn more slowly about what's missing.

**Churn rescue.** An account executive who calls a churning customer can save accounts that would otherwise cancel silently. Our churn intervention is purely passive (the cancellation flow asks why; we act on the patterns).

We've accepted these trade-offs. At our current stage and team size, a sales motion would require a full-time salesperson we don't have. The PLG model scales with the product, not with headcount.

## The future motion

At some future scale — probably when a significant portion of Scoutr revenue comes from professional and business tier accounts — a light sales layer makes sense. Not cold outbound, but inbound sales support: responding to enterprise inquiries, offering expanded demos for organisations with multiple users, and proactively reaching out to high-value accounts that have been on trial for 12+ days.

We're not there yet. For now, the product does the selling.
