We've been asked more than once about a fourth venture. Is it coming? What would it be? Are we working on something?

Honest answer: we're always generating ideas. We run a lightweight validation process on the ones that pass the initial filter. Nothing has passed the full process yet.

Here's how we think about new ventures in a portfolio model, and what the current criteria are.

## The idea generation process

We don't do formal brainstorming sessions. New venture ideas come from three sources:

**Problems we encounter while running the current products.** Scoutr's users make decisions about what to buy, where to source it, and how to price it. The tools they use for those decisions are often manual, slow, or absent. Every manual step in a professional workflow is a potential product.

**Industries where the Construx pattern applies.** We've built the infrastructure for AI-assisted professional tools: fast LLM calls, vector search, structured outputs, payment integration. The same infrastructure that powers Scoutr could power a similar tool for a different professional resale market — Amazon FBA, vintage clothing, electronics, car parts. We see these opportunities regularly.

**Conversations with users.** The most direct signal. When multiple Scoutr users ask "do you do anything for [thing]?" — that's a market question worth taking seriously.

## The initial filter

Most ideas die at the initial filter. The questions:

**Is this market real and is it professional?** We build for professionals who pay for tools that make them money. Consumer products, free tier-dependent products, and ad-supported products don't fit our model.

**Can AI create a meaningful advantage?** If the product could be built in 2015 without AI and would be essentially the same, it's not for us. AI should be structurally necessary — enabling something that wasn't possible before, not just automating something that was already doable.

**Is the market large enough?** We're a small team. We're not trying to build a billion-dollar company. But the market needs to be large enough to sustain a product that can be priced professionally. "Large enough" for us means: thousands of professional users willing to pay £10-100/month.

**Do we have an insight or a distribution advantage?** Walking into a market cold with no advantage is a bad bet. We have advantages in: AI integration, fast development velocity, and the Construx community/audience built through the journal and existing products. A new venture needs to leverage at least one of these.

## The 48-hour validation

Ideas that pass the initial filter go through a 48-hour validation process:

1. **User research (6 hours):** Find 5-10 potential users and have honest conversations. Not "would you use this?" (everyone says yes) but "how do you currently solve this problem?" and "what would make you pay for something better?"

2. **Competitive scan (4 hours):** What exists? Why do professionals use it or not? Where are the gaps?

3. **Technical feasibility (4 hours):** Is this buildable with our current stack? Does it require infrastructure we don't have?

4. **Unit economics model (2 hours):** What's the pricing model? What are the AI costs? At what user volume does it break even?

5. **Decision (final 2 hours):** Build, shelve, or kill.

Most ideas reach "shelve" — good idea, not the right time, or we can't validate user demand quickly enough. "Kill" is rare but happens when the competitive landscape makes the idea non-viable.

## What we're looking at currently

We're watching two categories:

**Adjacent professional resale markets.** Scoutr's core competency (AI-assisted profitability analysis for resellers) could apply to other resale markets. Each market is different in detail but similar in structure. We've had preliminary conversations with professionals in two of these markets.

**Tools for The Hyve's user base.** The Hyve workspace users are collaborative knowledge workers. The tools they use alongside The Hyve — project management, documentation, analysis — are largely not AI-native. There's a potential opportunity in the intersection of collaborative workspace and AI-native tooling that The Hyve doesn't currently occupy.

Neither is ready to commit to. Both are interesting.

## The criterion we won't compromise

One criterion applies to every potential fourth venture regardless of the market: it needs to be genuinely better than existing alternatives, not just AI-flavoured.

The landscape is full of products that use AI as a selling point without meaningfully improving on what came before. Users are sophisticated enough to notice this quickly. Our existing products are successful because the AI creates a real quality difference — Scoutr's analysis is faster and more accurate than manual eBay research; The Marqet's search finds things that keyword search misses; The Hyve's agent actually remembers team context.

Whatever comes fourth needs to meet the same bar. AI for the sake of it is not sufficient.

When something clears that bar — we'll build it.
