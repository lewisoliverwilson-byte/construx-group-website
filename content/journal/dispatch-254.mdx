Two-sided marketplaces have an activation problem. You need sellers to build liquidity. Sellers won't come without buyers. Buyers won't come without sellers. This is the classic chicken-and-egg problem, and the only solution is to hand-wave one side into existence first.

We decided to solve the seller side first. Here's why, and how.

## Why sellers first

The Marqet is a reseller marketplace — people who source products from one market to sell in another. The buyer side (people purchasing the products) is largely passive: they see inventory, they decide whether to buy. The seller side is active: they have to onboard, list, manage, and fulfil.

Sellers are the harder activation. If we acquired buyers first, we'd have a marketplace with no inventory. An empty shelf kills conversion faster than anything. We needed listings before we could meaningfully acquire buyers.

The second reason: sellers have a direct financial incentive to make the marketplace work. A buyer who finds nothing will leave and never come back. A seller who invested time building their storefront has skin in the game.

## The three-step onboarding flow

We compressed onboarding to three steps, each with a clear outcome.

**Step 1: Account and KYC.** We use Stripe Identity for identity verification. This is non-negotiable for a marketplace handling money — regulators require it, and it signals trust to buyers. The UX is: upload ID photo, selfie, done in under two minutes. We verified 94% of applicants on first attempt.

**Step 2: Payout setup.** Sellers connect their bank account via Stripe Connect before they can list anything. This is counterintuitive — asking people to link their bank before they've sold a single item. But sellers who complete bank setup are 3× more likely to list within 48 hours than those who skip it. Making it mandatory removed the "I'll do it when I make a sale" deferral that killed our early conversion.

**Step 3: First listing.** The final gating step. A seller with a verified account and payout setup who hasn't listed anything is an incomplete activation. We guide them directly to the listing creation flow from the onboarding confirmation screen. First listing completion is the activation event.

Time from signup to first listing live: median 22 minutes for our first 50 sellers.

## The listing wizard

First-time listing creation is where onboarding most commonly stalls. We invested heavily in reducing friction here.

The wizard is five steps:
1. Paste the source URL (Scoutr integration handles ASIN extraction, price fetching, and autofills the listing with the product data)
2. Set your sell price (we show the Scoutr margin estimate inline)
3. Add photos (drag and drop, auto-compressed, presigned R2 upload)
4. Set fulfilment terms (handling time, shipping options)
5. Review and publish

The Scoutr integration is the key win. A seller pastes a Keepa or Amazon URL and we pre-fill the product title, description, and category from our product database. They're not starting from a blank form — they're reviewing and adjusting pre-filled data. This halved listing creation time in our A/B test.

## The referral loop

We gave each seller a referral link after their first listing went live. For every seller they referred who completed their first listing, the referrer got reduced commission for 90 days.

This was the growth lever. Resellers know other resellers. The first 50 sellers brought in 23 referrals organically. The referral rate improved when we changed the incentive from a flat credit to a commission reduction — referrers care more about ongoing savings than a one-time payment.

## What almost killed us: KYC rejection rates

Our initial KYC flow had a 31% first-attempt rejection rate. The main failure modes:
- Expired ID documents (5%)
- Poor photo quality in low-light conditions (18%)
- ID in a language Stripe Identity didn't support at the time (8%)

We added pre-submission guidance (photo quality tips, supported document types) and a retry flow that showed the specific failure reason. Rejection rate dropped to 6%.

The unsupported language issue required a manual review path — applicants with non-supported documents go to a manual queue, which we review within 24 hours. This is still a small percentage but required us to build a lightweight admin tool for the review workflow.

## Seller health scoring

After 50 sellers were live, we introduced a seller health score — a composite of listing accuracy (do the listings match what buyers receive), fulfilment speed, dispute rate, and return rate. This scores visible to buyers on the seller profile page.

The score serves two functions: buyers can filter by high-health sellers, and it creates seller incentive to maintain quality. Sellers with health scores below a threshold get a warning and access to resources for improvement. If the score doesn't recover in 30 days, the account is reviewed.

We haven't had to suspend any accounts yet — the warning mechanism alone has been enough. Sellers who see their score drop take corrective action.

## The 60-day view

At day 60: 50 active sellers, 312 live listings, first buyer transactions. Sell-through rate on listed inventory: 18% in the first two weeks of the marketplace being open to buyers.

The number we're watching: repeat seller listing rate. Sellers who create a second listing within 30 days of their first have a 78% 90-day retention rate. Sellers who don't create a second listing have a 12% 90-day retention rate. The first listing is necessary; the second listing is the indicator that the seller is genuinely activated.

Our job now is to make the path to a second listing obvious and easy — better inventory management tools, repricing suggestions from Scoutr, and a clearer view of what's selling in the marketplace.
