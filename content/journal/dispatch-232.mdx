We spent three weeks in October running a cold outbound experiment for The Marqet. 200 emails to targeted prospects. A specific offer. A clear call to action. Here's what happened.

## The hypothesis

The Marqet is a marketplace for AI professionals. The buyer side of the marketplace — businesses that want to hire AI professionals — is the demand side we need to grow. We have decent supply (50+ sellers); we need buyers.

The hypothesis: businesses that have publicly signalled AI investment interest (job postings for AI roles, press mentions of AI initiatives, LinkedIn posts about AI strategy) would be receptive to a direct email offering curated access to AI freelancers on a no-platform-fee trial.

## The methodology

200 companies, selected by:
1. Companies posting LinkedIn job descriptions mentioning "prompt engineering", "AI workflow", or "LLM implementation" in the last 60 days
2. Tech companies with 10-50 employees (large enough to have AI needs, small enough that a decision-maker gets individual emails)
3. UK-based (geographic targeting for initial outreach given GBP pricing)

The email:

> **Subject:** AI freelancers without the overhead
>
> Hi [name],
>
> I saw [company] is hiring for [AI role]. If you need work done in the meantime — or alongside a hire — The Marqet has vetted AI professionals available for project work.
>
> We're waiving platform fees for the first 3 months for companies we invite directly. No commitment needed — just browse what's available.
>
> [Link to relevant category page]
>
> Lewis

Personalised: each email referenced the specific AI role or LinkedIn post that surfaced the company. Sent from a real address (lewis@construxgroup.io), not a tool.

## The results

- 200 emails sent
- 23% open rate (46 opens)
- 7 replies — 6 polite declines, 1 "interested, send more info"
- The interested prospect: a 15-person fintech company looking for help automating their compliance workflow
- Outcome: 1 discovery call. No transaction.

Total result: 0 conversions from 200 targeted, personalised emails.

## Why it didn't work

**Wrong timing.** Companies posting AI job descriptions are trying to hire employees, not freelancers. The mindset is "we want to build internal capability," not "we want to use external freelancers." These aren't the same need, and we targeted the signal for the wrong need.

**Wrong channel for the purchase decision.** Hiring a freelancer for AI work is a decision made by a technical manager or CTO. Cold email to a generic company email address (or even a named contact) doesn't reach the person who has the budget authority and the specific problem.

**Not urgent enough.** "Browse what's available" is a low-urgency CTA. The person reads the email, thinks "maybe, sometime," and never returns. Cold email converts best when the problem is acute: "our server is down," "we're shipping in 3 days and need help." Finding AI freelancers is not acute.

**Trust deficit.** A cold email from a platform the recipient has never heard of asking them to pay for services through an escrow system is high friction. The trust required to put money through a new platform doesn't come from an email; it comes from referral, reputation, or considerable inbound research.

## What we learned

The one response that came close to converting was from someone who found the journal post about The Marqet's launch two weeks before we emailed them. They already knew who we were. The email was a trigger for action they were already considering.

This is the pattern that works: warm outbound to people who've already found you through inbound content. Not cold outbound to strangers.

The unit economics also don't work. 200 personalised emails took 15 hours of research and writing. For 0 conversions. Even if we'd gotten 2-3 conversions, the time cost per conversion is too high relative to what journal content achieves with the same time investment.

## The channel we're using instead

We're not doing outbound for The Marqet buyer side. Instead:

**Referral from sellers.** Sellers who complete a successful transaction have a strong incentive to refer their existing clients. A client who's already worked with a seller trusts the seller's recommendation. The transaction happens through The Marqet, not through a direct engagement.

**Journal discovery.** Content about what AI professionals can do for businesses — not "use The Marqet" content, but genuinely useful content about specific AI applications — attracts buyers who are researching their options.

**Case studies.** When a transaction completes successfully, write about it (with permission). "How [company type] saved [X hours/£Y] using an AI professional from The Marqet" converts buyers who have the same problem.

The common thread: let buyers come to us when they have the need, rather than interrupting them when we think they might have the need. Cold outbound failed because we were guessing at the need. The alternatives work because the buyer self-identifies.

## When outbound would be right

Cold outbound isn't categorically wrong. It works when:
- The buyer's need is clearly defined and urgent
- You have strong personalisation that demonstrates you understand their specific situation
- The ask is small and low-commitment (free trial, 15-minute call, not "put money through our platform")
- The trust gap is bridged by a warm introduction or existing reputation

None of these applied in October. We might revisit outbound once The Marqet has enough case studies and testimonials that a cold email can credibly reference them. "X company got [result] in 3 weeks" is a different proposition than "browse what's available."

The experiment was worth running. Three weeks and 200 emails is a cheap way to test a hypothesis. The hypothesis was wrong. Now we know.
